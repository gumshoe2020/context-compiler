Step 3b — CLAIMS FIRST (claims come first so that errors are easy to identify and correct):
Before the narrative, write a section titled exactly "## Claims" — the complete list of load-bearing facts the rollup asserts, one per line, in this exact shape:
  - C1. <subject> | <predicate> | <object> — <YYYY-MM-DD> — <source filename> [— quote: "<≤20 words verbatim from the source>"]
The three fields are separated by " | " (space, pipe, space) and nothing else may contain " | "; a line without exactly two " | " delimiters is rejected as malformed. Example: "- C7. Sam | chose | a paper ledger over a spreadsheet — 2026-09-24 — 2026-09-24-0f1e2d3c4b5a.md — quote: \"the paper ledger stays\"". Write a replaced claim as "- C8. (superseded by C7) Riley | recommended | a spreadsheet — …" (currency) or "- C8. (corrected by C7) Riley | recommended | a spreadsheet — …" (reversal: C8 was wrong when written).
Rules: direction fixed and explicit (who corrected whom, who decided, who claimed — subject, then an active-voice predicate, then object; never passive voice such as "was corrected by"); one source filename per claim, a primary entry, never another rollup; a date on every claim; number them C1, C2, … ; every replaced claim stays in the list with one of two markers: "(corrected by Cn)" when the old claim was WRONG when written — a reversal — and "(superseded by Cn)" when it was true then and has merely been replaced since (currency). Every "(corrected by Cn)" pair MUST be paired with exactly one contradicts relation in Step 4, from the old claim's entity to the new one, with "claim": "Cn"; a "(superseded by Cn)" pair gets no negative edge. contradicts is never a quota: if no reversal was resolved, write no contradicts relation. Every proposed graph relation in Step 4 MUST cite the claim number it derives from (a "claim": "Cn" field on the relation object). The "Current consolidated truth" section is then the narrative rendering of the Claims and may not assert anything load-bearing that has no claim beneath it. Aim for 8–25 claims; if a fact is not worth a claim line, it is not worth the narrative either. The fidelity watcher and any future correction command work at the claim level.
QUOTES MUST CARRY THE OBJECT (this is the validator's own rule, and a rollup with one claim that fails it is rejected whole): a quote is copied verbatim from the claim's source entry, and it supports its claim only if (a) the quote itself contains one of the object's distinctive words, or (b) the source text within 600 characters on either side of the quote contains both the subject and one of those words. The object's distinctive words are its proper nouns and number-like tokens (names, "12:4", "90-day") when it has any; otherwise its content words (four letters or more, not common words such as "about", "which", "their"). A capitalised word right after "and", "or", a comma, a period, a semicolon, a colon, "!", "?", a dash or "/", or one that begins with "(", "[", a quotation mark or "/", counts as a name only if it is capitalised elsewhere in the claim (in "Article 12:4 and Whitcombe", "Whitcombe" is not a name, so the quote must carry "Article" or "12:4"). A word that is also in the subject never counts; when every content word of the object is also in the subject, those words count, and the quote must also carry a content word of the predicate. An object must hold a name, a number or a word of four letters or more that is not a common word, or no quote can support its claim: such a claim is rejected as "Cn: the object has no word a quote could carry", and only rewriting the claim mends it. Words match whole and in any case ("port" never matches "report"; "Sam's" counts as "Sam"). A quote that names only who (the subject) supports its claim only when the text around it (600 characters on either side) carries one of the object's words; without that it does not, however true the claim is. So choose each quote for the object's words: quote the phrase that names the thing claimed. In the pinned examples below (source, then a claim that passes, a claim that fails, and a claim that passes by the text around its quote), C1 passes because its quote carries "Maplewood", a proper noun of its object; C2 fails because its quote names only Sam, the subject, and neither it nor the text around it carries "Article" or "12:4"; C3 passes although its quote names only Sam, because the text around the quote carries "Maplewood".

SPECIFICS — CITE BY ID, NEVER RESTATE: an exact fact the distiller filed as a specific (a date, name, number, decision, commitment, or someone's exact words) is listed under "LIVE SPECIFICS" at the end of this message with its claim id. A rollup never paraphrases one: a claim with the same subject and predicate that cites the source named after it (or an entry that source supersedes, or the rollup that carries it) carries its value exactly as its object (the same words from an unrelated source are a claim of their own), and the narrative cites it by its claim id and copies the value exactly (e.g. "the inspection is on May 21 (c_0123456789ab)"). When you cite a specific's handle in prose, quote its value exactly, in quotation marks, next to the handle. A rollup that states a different value for a live specific fails validation. Ideas, reasoning and themes (concepts) are what a rollup consolidates in its own words.
